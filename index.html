I am a Ph.D. Candidate in History and Early Modern Studies interested primarily in the intellectual history of Southeast Asia and the Indian Ocean. My training is in Indonesian history and Malay manuscript studies. Through my dissertation project, I examine texts produced by Minangkabau itinerants and migrants who had scattered all over colonial Southeast Asia. I’m especially curious about how they mobilised literary form to negotiate complex social, political, theological, and economic worlds.

Translation is an important part of my academic practice, and I see this to be one of the most key (and incidentally also most fun) parts of my work as a scholar. It is my ambition that my work as a textual scholar might bring Classical Malay texts to as broad and as thoughtful an audience as possible.

I grew up in Singapore, and subsequently earned an A.B. in History from Brown University, as well as an M.A. in Southeast Asian Studies from Chulalongkorn University. I was named a 2023 recipient of the Singapore Teaching and Academic Research Talent (START) awards, and will be joining Nanyang Technological University as an Assistant Professor of History in 2030.

Until the end of 2027, I am based in Leiden, The Netherlands, where I am a visiting researcher at Leiden University and an affiliated fellow of the KITLV.

The following things currently occupy my waking mind:

  • Malay poetry and their manuscript forms
  • Histories of political thought in early modern Southeast Asia
  • The social lives of texts in the Malay and Islamicate worlds – how are texts themselves participants in their social and material worlds, rather than simply being mirrors unto them?
  • How might we speak of Islam in its capacity as a discursive tradition that has given rise to Malay aesthetic and poetic forms?
